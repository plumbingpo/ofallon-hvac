Signs Your Air Conditioner Is Failing in O'Fallon, MO

Home / Blog / Signs Your Air Conditioner Is Failing in O'Fallon, MO

As the warmest months approach here in O'Fallon, MO, our air conditioners become hard-working systems of our home comfort. These units operate constantly to keep our indoor spaces cool and comfortable. But like any mechanical system, air conditioners aren't designed to last forever and will eventually begin to show signs of wear and tear, indicating that a major repair or replacement might be on the horizon. Being aware of these symptoms can save you from a surprise system failure on the hottest day of the year, and potentially avoid more extensive problems in the long run. Here at O'Fallon HVAC, we want to help you understand what to look out for.

Reduced Cooling Performance: A Lack of Consistent Cooling?

One of the most noticeable and irritating signs that your air conditioner is struggling is a drop in its cooling capability. You might find yourself adjusting the thermostat lower and lower only to still feel warm or humid indoors. Uneven cooling throughout your house is a tell-tale sign. This isn't just about comfort; it indicates a problem within the system. There are multiple potential causes for this problem. It could be a depleted refrigerant charge, which means your system isn't able to absorb and release heat effectively. Refrigerant is the lifeblood of your AC. Another common culprit is a obstructed air filter, which restricts airflow and makes the unit work harder without achieving the desired cooling. Dirty evaporator coils can also be a factor, as they cannot efficiently transfer heat. The compressor itself, the engine of your AC, could be giving out. If you're experiencing poor temperature control, it's time to have a professional from O'Fallon HVAC diagnose the problem.

Strange Noises: When Your AC Sounds Off

Your air conditioner should operate with a relatively consistent, low hum. If you start hearing unusual or loud noises coming from your unit, it's a definite sign that something is amiss. Worn motor bearings are often the source of grinding or squealing sounds. These bearings, essential for the motor to turn freely, can wear down over time, leading to friction and eventual motor failure if not addressed. Repetitive clicking might signal a faulty relay or other electrical trouble. If you hear banging or clunking, be very concerned, as they might suggest a component that has come dislodged and is striking other parts. Don't delay in addressing these auditory warnings. Addressing these auditory clues promptly can prevent a small issue from becoming a costly repair.

Increased Energy Bills: An Unexpected Surge in Utility Costs

A subtle but significant sign of a struggling AC is a gradual rise in your electricity costs. If your bills are increasing without a change in thermostat settings, your AC unit might be working harder than it should to achieve the same level of cooling. This inefficiency can be caused by a variety of factors. As mentioned, a clogged air filter reduces airflow, forcing the fan motor to work overtime. If your ducts are leaking, you're cooling your attic instead of your home, meaning your AC has to run longer to maintain the desired temperature. An aging or improperly sized unit may simply be past its prime. Refrigerant leaks also force the system to work harder. The compressor and fan motors can become less efficient as they age. If your utility costs are climbing for no apparent reason, it's worth having O'Fallon HVAC inspect your AC unit to identify the source of the inefficiency. Our team can help you determine the most cost-effective solution.

Poor Airflow: Is Air Barely Coming Out of the Vents?

Proper airflow ensures consistent cooling and even temperatures across all rooms. If you notice that the air coming out of your vents is weak, or if some rooms have much lower air pressure than others, it's a clear warning of an underlying problem. Poor airflow can be attributed to multiple factors. A primary offender is a filter choked with dust and debris, which restricts the volume of air that can pass through your system. Obstructed return vents can impede the system's ability to pull air in. Beyond filters, issues with the fan motor itself can lead to reduced airflow. If the fan motor is weakening, it won't be able to push air through the ductwork effectively. Ductwork blockages or damage can severely limit airflow. Closed dampers in your duct system can lead to inconsistent airflow distribution. It's critical to resolve poor airflow to improve comfort and protect your AC from premature wear.

Excessive Moisture or Leaks: Is Your AC Leaking Water?

Some condensation is expected from an AC, but significant moisture or standing water around the unit is problematic. This is a unmistakable indicator that something is wrong. The most common cause of water leaks is a clogged condensate drain line. As your AC cools the air, it removes humidity, and this moisture collects in a drain pan and flows out through a condensate line. If this line becomes blocked by algae, dirt, or debris, the water will back up and overflow the pan. Water damage and electrical hazards are possible consequences. Beyond a clogged drain, a cracked or rusted drain pan can also cause leaks. In more severe cases, a refrigerant leak can also cause ice to form on the evaporator coils, which then melts and overflows the drain pan. If you see unexpected wetness around your system, it's important to call for service right away to prevent further damage. O'Fallon HVAC can diagnose the source of the leak and provide a lasting solution.

Frequent Cycling: When Your AC Can't Stay On

Your air conditioner should typically run in relatively consistent cycles, turning on to cool your home and then shutting off once the desired temperature is reached. If your AC starts turning on and off rapidly, a phenomenon called "short cycling," it's a serious indicator of a problem. When an AC short cycles, it fails to finish a complete cooling cycle, leading to inefficiency and system strain. This can be caused by several issues. If your AC is too big for your home, it might short cycle, cooling fast but not dehumidifying. Often, short cycling is due to low refrigerant, a dirty filter, or a faulty thermostat. A struggling compressor or an electrical fault can also be culprits. Short cycling, regardless of its origin, quickly degrades AC parts, leading to early failure and higher bills. If your AC is short cycling, it's not operating efficiently or effectively, and it needs professional attention.

Old Age: How Old Is Your AC Unit?

The age of your AC unit, even if it seems fine, is a major predictor of future issues. A central AC system generally has a lifespan of 10 to 15 years, though some last longer with diligent care. As your unit approaches or exceeds this age range, it becomes more prone to breakdowns and less efficient. Many older ACs use R-22, an obsolete refrigerant that's expensive to service. Components in older systems are naturally more worn from years of use. Though maintenance helps, eventually repairs become too frequent and expensive compared to replacing with a new unit. If your AC is over a decade old and showing symptoms, it's wise to consider replacement options. Investing in a new, energy-efficient AC can lead to considerable savings and improved comfort for the long term.
