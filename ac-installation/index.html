A new air conditioner should be a fifteen-year decision, and the installation day determines most of it. The industry's open secret is that a poorly installed system gives up a large slice of its rated efficiency before it ever cools a room: an oversized unit short-cycles, dehumidifies poorly, and leaves a O'Fallon house cold but clammy; an undersized one runs flat-out and never catches up on the worst afternoons. That is why O'Fallon HVAC starts every installation with a real load calculation, measuring your home's square footage, insulation, window exposure, and duct system, instead of matching the tonnage bolted to the wall by the last contractor.

Next comes choosing equipment, with no upselling. Efficiency is now rated in SEER2, and stepping up from a basic single-stage unit to a two-stage or variable-speed system brings quieter running, longer and gentler cycles, and much better humidity control. For some homes that is worth every dollar. For others it is not, and we will say so. The installation itself is where performance is won: the line set is sized correctly and pressure-tested, a deep vacuum is pulled and confirmed on a micron gauge, the charge is weighed in and checked against superheat and subcooling, the condensate drain is trapped, sloped, and protected by a float switch, and airflow is checked with static pressure readings so old ductwork does not choke the new system.

Every install ends with full commissioning. We measure the temperature split, record amp draws, set up the thermostat, and watch the system run complete cycles before calling it done. Permits are handled where required, and on replacement jobs we haul the old equipment away. The Benefits of Professional AC Installation

A new AC can blow cold on day one and still be headed for trouble. Moisture left in a line set that was never properly evacuated forms acid that slowly destroys the compressor. A refrigerant charge set by feel rather than by weight drains efficiency with every cycle. An oversized system, chosen without a load calculation, short-cycles itself into an early grave and never dries the air. Installation is where a system's future gets decided, and none of these errors reveal themselves until the repair bills and utility costs start climbing.

Our experienced technicians do the unglamorous steps that make equipment last: nitrogen pressure testing before evacuation, a micron-gauge-verified vacuum, charge set by weight and confirmed against measured superheat and subcooling, condensate drainage with a proper trap and overflow protection, and airflow verified with static pressure readings rather than a hand held over a register. Refrigerant handling itself is federally regulated, and the recovery and evacuation equipment involved is not a hardware-store purchase. Done right, an installation disappears into fifteen quiet years of cold air, and that is the outcome we commission every job to deliver.

AC Installation Prevention Tips

A little routine maintenance goes a long way toward avoiding a mid-season breakdown. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Address small issues early; a soft capacitor caught in April never becomes a July breakdown
  • Run a professional tune-up every spring to keep the charge and amp draws on spec
  • Change the filter on schedule from day one so the new coil never sees starved airflow
  • Use reasonable thermostat setpoints; giant setbacks force long recovery runs in peak heat
  • Flush the condensate line each spring so the float switch never has to save you
  • Hose off the outdoor unit occasionally and keep plants away from every side
  • Do not close vents in unused rooms; the system's airflow was balanced with all of them open
