From the hallway, a furnace that will not heat looks the same no matter what broke. Inside the cabinet, though, the sequence is strict: inducer on, pressure switch closed, igniter glowing, gas valve open, burners lit, flame proven, and finally the blower moving heat through the ducts. Most of the diagnosis comes down to finding the broken link. In O'Fallon homes, it tends to be the same handful of culprits: a flame sensor coated in oxide, a hot surface igniter cracked by thermal cycling, a pressure switch held open by a tired inducer or a plugged condensate trap, or a control board with a burned relay.

O'Fallon HVAC repairs furnaces by testing the sequence, not guessing at it. Our experienced technicians verify each stage with a meter rather than a hunch: igniter resistance, flame signal in microamps, pressure switch operation against actual inducer draft, gas valve voltage, and blower and capacitor health. Every visit includes the safety fundamentals on a fuel-burning appliance: heat exchanger inspection, leak-testing of gas connections, and flue draft verification, because the difference between a furnace problem and a furnace hazard is not visible from the thermostat. We measure temperature rise against the data plate before we leave so the fire and the airflow are matched the way the manufacturer intended.

Conventional or condensing, gas, propane, or electric, we work on them all, across every major brand, including the traps, drains, and dual pressure switches that come with high-efficiency models. The parts most likely to fail are stocked on every truck: igniters, flame sensors, pressure switches, capacitors, inducer assemblies, and blower motors. Warning Signs for Furnace Repair

The sooner you notice these red flags, the easier and cheaper the problem is to resolve. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Rust streaks, soot, or water around the cabinet or flue
  • A furnace that shuts off every few minutes before the house warms up
  • A bang or boom when the burners ignite late
  • A flame that burns soft and yellow rather than steady blue
  • Squealing or grinding from the blower compartment
  • The blower runs constantly but the air never warms
  • The igniter glows and clicks, yet no flame ever appears
  • Flames that start, last a few seconds, and go out again and again

Early Action Saves Money

The Benefits of Professional Furnace Repair

Gas, open flame, high voltage, and a flue share one cabinet in a furnace, and the most dangerous failure is the one you cannot see: a damaged heat exchanger that lets combustion gases into the air the blower circulates. Every morning that delayed ignition keeps booming at startup, that exchanger takes more stress. The diagnostic side has its own pitfalls. When a furnace trips its high limit, the switch itself may be bad, or the furnace may be protecting itself from a clogged filter or a failing blower. Replacing the switch when the real issue is airflow removes a safety device and leaves the actual problem in place.

At O'Fallon HVAC, no symptom gets treated in isolation. A meter tells us the flame signal, so we never condemn a good sensor while the real culprit, a cracked igniter or a weak gas valve, stays in place. We confirm draft at both the pressure switch and the flue, measure temperature rise against the data plate to flag airflow problems early, and test the gas side with a leak detector, not a guess. When we are done, the furnace has proven itself safe and correct through a full cycle, and if it is nearing the end of its useful life, we tell you honestly.

Maintenance Tips for Furnace Repair

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly heating and cooling problems down the road. Put these technician-approved steps to work around your property:

  • Clear boxes, lint, and above all chlorine-based cleaners out of the furnace area
  • Put a carbon monoxide detector on every level and test them monthly
  • Schedule service each fall to catch worn igniters and dirty sensors before cold weather arrives
  • Put in a fresh filter monthly through the heating season, the cheapest protection your furnace can get
  • Pay attention when the burners light, since a boom instead of a soft whoosh calls for service
  • Keep the condensate trap and drain clear on high-efficiency units to prevent lockouts
  • Make sure the furnace has clear combustion air; a packed utility room starves the burners
